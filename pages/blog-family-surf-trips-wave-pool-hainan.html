Family surf trips in a wave pool

Kids on gentle rollers, parents on real waves — the same pool, the same hour. Why a wave pool on a tropical island is the family surf holiday that actually works.

Published August 28, 2026
Read Time 6 min
Wave Settings ~20, gentle to barreling
Water Warm year-round, no crowds
Home/Blog/Family Surf Trips in a Wave Pool

Every surfing parent has run the maths on a family surf trip and watched it fall apart. The ocean doesn't care about your one week of school holidays: the swell arrives early or late, the shorebreak scares the kids, the lineup is crowded, and somebody spends the whole trip on the beach holding towels. A wave pool deletes every one of those problems at once. At the PerfectSwell® pool at Riyue Bay — the world's only wave pool on a tropical island — the waves run on a schedule, the water is warm, and around 20 wave settings mean a seven-year-old and a forty-seven-year-old can both score in the same hour.

Why a wave pool beats the ocean for learning

Learning to surf in the ocean is mostly not surfing. It's paddling, waiting, getting caught inside, and hoping the next wave is the right one. In the pool there are no rips, no currents and no crowds — just a predictable wave arriving where the coach said it would, when the coach said it would. That repetition is the whole trick: a kid gets many more wave attempts in one pool session than in days of ocean surfing, and every attempt is on the same wave, so the lesson from the last ride carries straight into the next one.

It's also easy on the nervous parent. The pool is contained and watchable — you can stand at the edge with a coffee and see every wave your kids catch, which is not something Riyue Bay's outside section will ever offer. If you want to know what the machine can actually do, from lake-flat to barreling, we've broken down the PerfectSwell system and its settings separately.

One pool, every ability level

The setting board is what makes this a genuine family trip rather than a kids' camp the adults endure. With around 20 wave settings, the pool can serve soft, crumbling rollers for first-timers, punchier open faces for improvers, and barrels and air sections for the best surfer in your family. The settings change between sets — so a session can be programmed like a menu: the kids' waves, then mum's waves, then the waves dad has been visualising since the flight.

Book it as a private session and the pool runs for your group only. No strangers in the lineup, no pressure, nobody hassling the slowest paddler in the family — every wave belongs to someone you brought with you. It's the same format our surf camps and group getaways use, scaled down to one family.

The kids get their waves, the parents get theirs, and everyone is back at the pool bar for lunch telling the same stories — that's the trip.

The rest of the family is covered too

A family trip lives or dies on the non-surfers' itinerary, and this is where Wanning over-delivers. Golfers in the family can play China's best links course at The Dunes, Shenzhou Peninsula, twenty-odd minutes from the pool. Everyone else has morning markets, boat-to-table seafood and Xinglong coffee plantations — plus a bean-to-bar chocolate workshop that is the single easiest win with kids on the island. Add Li culture inland, beach days on the bay, and when the season delivers, an ocean session at Riyue Bay's point breaks for the surfers who want a taste of the real thing.

The logistics are easier than you think

Hainan runs a 30-day visa-free policy for passport holders from the U.S., Australia, New Zealand, the UK, Canada and many other countries — no embassy queues with children's passports, no paperwork week. Fly into Haikou (HAK), with direct flights from Sydney and Melbourne, or into Sanya (SYX); from Europe, North America and most of Asia, connect via Hong Kong, Singapore, Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur or Seoul. The full route breakdown is in the getting here guide, and family-friendly resort options at Riyue Bay and Shenzhou Peninsula are covered in accommodation.

Good to Know

Family surf trips — FAQ

Is a wave pool a good place for kids to learn to surf?

It's arguably the best place. There are no rips, no currents and no crowds to navigate — just a predictable, gentle wave arriving on schedule in warm, waist-friendly water, with coaches in the pool. Kids get many more wave attempts per hour than they ever would in the ocean, which is what actually builds the skill.

Can parents and kids surf in the same session?

Yes. The PerfectSwell pool has around 20 wave settings, from soft beginner rollers to barreling waves, and the setting can change from one set to the next. In a private session the pool runs waves for your group only, so the kids' waves and the parents' waves share the same hour.

What can non-surfing family members do in Wanning?

Plenty. The pool is easy to watch from the side, and around it Wanning offers golf (including China's best links course), morning markets and seafood, Xinglong coffee plantations, a bean-to-bar chocolate workshop, Li culture inland and beach days at Riyue Bay. We build the non-surf side of the trip alongside the sessions.

Do we need visas for a family trip to Hainan?

For most of our guests, no. Hainan offers 30-day visa-free entry for passport holders from the U.S., Australia, New Zealand, the UK, Canada and many other countries. Fly into Haikou (HAK) — with direct flights from Sydney and Melbourne — or Sanya (SYX), or connect via Hong Kong, Singapore, Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur or Seoul.
