Surf camps at a private wave pool

The same wave, on repeat, with a coach watching every ride. Why running your surf camp at a private wave pool compresses months of ocean progression into days.

Published August 29, 2026
Read Time 6 min
Format Private sessions, your group only
Wave Settings ~20, gentle to barreling
Home/Blog/Surf Camps at a Private Wave Pool

Every surf camp makes the same two promises: waves and coaching. The second one is easy to keep. The first is a gamble — on swell, on wind, on tide, on how many strangers show up at the same break. A camp at a private wave pool keeps both promises before anyone gets on a plane. At the PerfectSwell® pool at Riyue Bay — the world's only wave pool on a tropical island — the waves are guaranteed, the water is warm year-round, and in a private session the pool runs for your group only.

Repetition is the whole secret

Surfing is brutally hard to practise because the ocean never serves the same wave twice. You get one attempt at a takeoff, then paddle back out and wait for a different wave to try a different lesson on. Coaches call it the feedback-loop problem: by the time the next comparable wave arrives, the last ride is a memory.

A wave pool deletes the problem. The machine produces the same wave, on demand — so a drill gets dozens of attempts in a single session, each one starting from an identical takeoff in an identical spot. Miss the section? The same section comes back in the next set. Your coach stands metres away, sees every ride from start to finish, and the correction lands while the wave is still fresh. That loop — attempt, feedback, identical retry — is what a camp is buying, and no ocean on earth can sell it.

One settings board, every level in your camp

The pool has around 20 wave settings, from soft, crumbling rollers up to barreling waves with sections built for airs — we've broken the full menu down in our PerfectSwell explainer. For a camp, the settings board is a curriculum. Beginners spend the morning on gentle waves until pop-ups are automatic; improvers step up a notch to work on trim and turns; the sharpest surfers in the group finish on the settings that barrel.

Because the setting can change from one set to the next, a mixed-ability camp doesn't have to split up or compromise. Nobody is out of their depth and nobody is bored — the wave meets each surfer where they are, then moves the goalposts on schedule.

Camps that work in this format

Progression camps

The classic learn-faster trip: a group of surfers, a coaching team, and a block of private sessions programmed around specific goals — first green waves, first cutback, first barrel. Wave-count maths does the heavy lifting: sessions here deliver more rides per hour than most surfers log in weeks of ocean surfing.

Crews and group getaways

A private session is the best version of surfing with your friends: no strangers in the lineup, no jockeying for sets, every wave belonging to someone you know. Book a run of them and the trip becomes a rolling session with a scoreboard.

Teams and clubs

For boardriders clubs and junior squads, a repeatable wave is a training tool the ocean can't match — the same section, every attempt, until the manoeuvre sticks. Program the settings, run the drills, film everything from the pool's edge.

An ocean camp promises coaching and hopes for conditions. A wave pool camp guarantees both — the swell is on the settings board.

Between sessions, the island shows up

A camp isn't only pool hours, and Wanning fills the gaps well. When the season delivers swell, Riyue Bay's ocean breaks are right there for a real-world exam of what the pool taught. Golfers can slip away to The Dunes at Shenzhou Peninsula, China's best links course. Rest days run through morning markets, seafood and Xinglong coffee country, and accommodation at Riyue Bay and Shenzhou Peninsula keeps the whole camp within easy reach of the pool.

Good to Know

Wave pool surf camps — FAQ

Why run a surf camp at a wave pool instead of the ocean?

Because the waves are guaranteed and repeatable. An ocean camp can promise coaching but not conditions; a wave pool camp delivers the same wave on demand, so every drill gets dozens of attempts and the coach watches every single ride from a few metres away. There are no flat days, no crowds and no time lost paddling around a lineup.

Can a camp with mixed ability levels train together?

Yes — that's the point of the settings board. The PerfectSwell pool has around 20 wave settings, from gentle beginner rollers to barreling waves, and the setting can change from one set to the next. A single session can serve first-timers, improvers and advanced surfers in turn, so nobody in the group is surfing the wrong wave.

Does our group get the pool to ourselves?

In a private session, yes — the pool runs waves for your group only. No strangers in the water, no queue for sets, and the wave menu is programmed around your camp's goals. Tell us your group size and ability mix and we'll shape the sessions, coaching and schedule around it.

When should we schedule a camp, and do we need visas?

The pool runs year-round in warm water, so a camp works in any season. For most guests no visa is needed: Hainan offers 30-day visa-free entry for passport holders from the U.S., Australia, New Zealand, the UK, Canada and many other countries. Fly into Haikou (HAK) — with direct flights from Sydney and Melbourne — or Sanya (SYX), or connect via Hong Kong, Singapore, Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur or Seoul.
