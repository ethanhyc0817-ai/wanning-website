How to get to Riyue Bay: flights, airports & the coastal drive

One international flight, one driver, one coastal drive — that's the whole journey to the world's only tropical island wave pool. Here's every route, airport by airport.

Published August 23, 2026
Read Time 6 min
Airports Haikou (HAK) · Sanya (SYX)
Transfer Included · 1–2 hrs, coastal road
Home/Blog/How to Get to Riyue Bay

Riyue Bay sounds remote — a surf town on the east coast of a tropical Chinese island. In practice, getting here is one of the easiest long-haul trips you can book: a single flight into Hainan, then a private van down a coastal highway to the PerfectSwell® wave pool. No domestic connections, no taxis, no apps. This guide covers the two airports, the direct routes from around the world, and what happens after you land.

Step one: pick your airport — Haikou (HAK) or Sanya (SYX)

Hainan has two international gateways, and both work for Riyue Bay. The right one is simply whichever has the better flight from your city.

Haikou Meilan International (HAK)

The island's main international gateway, in the north. Most long-haul flights land here — including the direct flights from Sydney and Melbourne — and it has the deepest schedule of international routes. From the arrivals hall it's about a two-hour drive down the east coast to Riyue Bay.

Sanya Phoenix International (SYX)

The southern airport, in Hainan's resort zone. Fewer long-haul routes, but the shortest drive to the Surf Pool — roughly one hour. If your connection lands you in Sanya, you'll be checking in before your board bag has settled.

Where the flights come from

Australia & New Zealand

The easiest long-haul routing on this list: Sydney → Haikou direct, Melbourne → Haikou direct, and Auckland → Haikou direct. Leave home in the evening, land on a tropical island the next day, surf that afternoon if you're keen.

Europe

London and Brussels have direct flights to Haikou; from other European cities it's a one-stop trip, typically through Hong Kong, Singapore or a mainland hub.

Southeast & East Asia

This is where the schedule gets dense. Singapore flies to Haikou daily, and there are direct routes from Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur, Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City and Seoul — Seoul serves both Haikou and Sanya. From Tokyo, Osaka or Taipei, connect via Shanghai or Hong Kong.

North America

One stop is all it takes. From the West Coast, connect through Hong Kong, Seoul or Tokyo; from the East Coast, through Hong Kong, Beijing or Shanghai. We help every guest pick the cleanest connection when they book.

And the paperwork? For most passports there isn't any. Hainan admits travellers from 86 countries — including Australia, the U.S., the UK, Canada and New Zealand — visa-free for up to 30 days. We covered the policy in detail in our Hainan visa-free travel guide.

Step two: the pickup and the coastal drive

Forward us your e-ticket and the rest is handled. An English-speaking driver waits in the arrivals hall with your name on a sign, and a WhatsApp number is shared in advance so you always have a real person to reach. We track your flight live — if you're delayed, the driver shifts with the new arrival time, and late-night arrivals are fine because the resort accepts check-ins around the clock.

The vehicle is a modern business van — Mercedes V-Class or equivalent — with Wi-Fi, USB charging, bottled water and space for boards and luggage, seating up to six guests per vehicle. Bringing multiple boards or a bigger crew? Tell us in advance and we size the vehicle accordingly.

The drive itself is a highlight. Hainan's east coast highway runs straight along the South China Sea — beaches on one side, coconut groves and buffalo fields on the other. It's the road you'd drive for the view; the airport just happens to be at one end of it.

What's waiting at the end of the road

Your trip coordinator meets you in the lobby of your resort, and the pool is a minute's walk from your room. From there the island opens up: the world's only wave pool on a tropical island with around twenty settings from beginner rollers to barrels — see the surf session for how a session runs — plus Riyue Bay's ocean break a short walk down the beach. Golfers can pair morning waves with afternoon rounds at The Dunes at Shenzhou Peninsula or Blackstone at Mission Hills — the full pairing is on our golf page — and the local seafood and Xinglong coffee fill everything in between.

Good to Know

Getting to Riyue Bay — FAQ

Which airport is better for Riyue Bay — Haikou (HAK) or Sanya (SYX)?

Whichever has the better flight for you. Haikou Meilan (HAK) has the most international routes, including the direct flights from Sydney and Melbourne, and is about a two-hour drive from Riyue Bay. Sanya Phoenix (SYX) is the shorter drive at roughly one hour. Both work — a private transfer from either airport is included in every package.

Are there direct flights from Australia to Hainan?

Yes. Sydney and Melbourne both have direct flights to Haikou (HAK), and Auckland has a direct route as well. You can leave home in the evening and be on the island the next day — no mainland China connection required.

Is the airport transfer to Riyue Bay included?

Yes. Every package includes round-trip private transfers between either airport and the resort — a modern business van with Wi-Fi, bottled water and space for boards, seating up to six per vehicle. An English-speaking driver meets you in the arrivals hall with your name on a sign, and we track your flight live in case of delays.

Do I need a visa to fly into Hainan?

Most travellers don't. Passport holders from the United States, Australia, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, Canada and many other countries enter Hainan visa-free for up to 30 days — no application, no embassy queue. We confirm your eligibility when you book.
