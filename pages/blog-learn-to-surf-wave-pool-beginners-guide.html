Learn to surf in a wave pool: a beginner’s guide

Your first wave shouldn’t be a matter of luck. How complete beginners stand up fast on the gentle settings of the PerfectSwell® pool at Riyue Bay — warm water, soft boards, same wave on repeat.

Published September 1, 2026
Read Time 6 min
Experience Needed None — beginner settings
Water Warm & tropical, year-round
Home/Blog/Learn to Surf in a Wave Pool

Ask anyone who learned to surf in the ocean how their first session went and you'll hear the same story: two hours of paddling, a mouthful of saltwater, and maybe — maybe — one wobbly ride they can't quite remember. Learning to surf has always been slow because the classroom keeps moving. A wave pool fixes exactly that. At Riyue Bay in Wanning, Hainan, the PerfectSwell® pool serves the same friendly wave, in the same spot, on schedule — and that changes how fast a beginner learns more than anything else you can buy, book or pack.

Why a wave pool is the best classroom in surfing

Surfing is a repetition sport trapped inside an unpredictable environment. Pop-ups, stance, weight over the front foot — none of it is complicated, but in the ocean you might get five real attempts in a session, each on a slightly different wave. In the pool, the variables are gone. The wave breaks where it broke last time. It arrives when it's supposed to. Nobody shoulder-hops you. Every attempt starts from the same place, so every attempt teaches you something the last one set up.

The machine matters here. This PerfectSwell® system has roughly 20 wave settings, from soft, slow rollers built for first-timers up to barrels and air sections for advanced surfers — our wave pool explainer walks through the full range. For a beginner that means the session starts on the gentlest setting and only moves up when you do. The wave grows with you, not the other way around.

Your first session, step by step

Before you get wet

Sessions start on land: the pop-up, where your feet should land, how to fall safely. Because Hainan is a tropical island — and this is the world's only wave pool on one — the water is warm year-round, so you'll do all of this in boardshorts or a swimsuit and a rash guard, not a thick wetsuit. Everything you need is at the pool — boards, wetsuits and rash guards are all in the rental quiver.

The board

Beginners start on a soft-top: big, buoyant, forgiving of feet that land in the wrong place. You don't need to own one — the rental quiver at Riyue Bay runs from soft-tops and longboards through performance shortboards, so as your rides get longer you can size down, sometimes within the same trip.

In the water

The first waves are whitewater rides: catch the rolling foam, pop up, ride it straight. Because the next wave behaves like the last one, corrections actually stick — hands too far forward on one attempt becomes hands in the right place on the next. Most beginners are standing within their first session, and riding the open face of an unbroken wave — the moment surfing becomes surfing — comes far sooner than it would in the sea.

In the ocean, a beginner's biggest opponent isn't the wave — it's the waiting. Take away the waiting and what's left is just learning.

From pool waves to ocean waves

The pool isn't the end of the road; it's the on-ramp. Riyue Bay is also the home of Chinese surfing, with real South China Sea swell breaking a few minutes from the pool deck. Once your pop-up is automatic, an ocean session at Riyue Bay is the natural next step, and the surf calendar shows what the sea is doing when. Skills built on repeatable waves transfer directly: you paddle into the ocean already knowing how to stand, which is the part that eats most people's first year. Our pool vs ocean comparison digs into how the two teach differently.

Making a trip of it

Learning to surf is a better excuse for a holiday than most. Hainan offers 30-day visa-free entry for passport holders from the U.S., Australia, New Zealand, the UK, Canada and many other countries, and getting here is simple — direct flights from Sydney and Melbourne to Haikou, or easy connections through Hong Kong, Singapore, Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur and Seoul; the details are in our getting here guide. Accommodation by the bay puts you minutes from the pool, and between sessions there's Wanning's food and Xinglong coffee to work through. Travelling as a crew? The pool can be booked privately, which for a group of beginners means a session with nobody in the water but your friends.

How fast will I actually progress?

Faster than you expect, and the reason is simple arithmetic: wave count. A beginner who catches thirty waves in a pool session has had more real practice than most ocean learners get in a week. Stack a few of those sessions back to back over a long weekend and you can arrive having never touched a board and leave trimming along an open face. That's the whole promise of learning here — not a shortcut around the work, just far more of the work packed into every hour you're in the water.

Good to Know

Learning to surf in a wave pool — FAQ

Is a wave pool a good place to learn to surf?

It's arguably the best place. In the ocean, beginners spend most of a session paddling, waiting and hoping; in the PerfectSwell® pool at Riyue Bay, the wave arrives on schedule, breaks in the same spot and behaves the same way every time. That repetition — the same take-off, again and again — is what turns wobbly first attempts into confident rides quickly.

Do I need any surfing experience to book a wave pool session?

No. The pool has roughly 20 wave settings, and the gentlest of them are soft, slow, rolling waves made for first-timers. Tell us your level when you book and the session runs on settings that match it — complete beginners are welcome.

What board should a beginner use in the wave pool?

A soft-top — big, stable and forgiving. You don't need to bring one: the rental quiver at Riyue Bay runs from soft-tops and longboards through performance shortboards, so you can start on a soft-top and size down as you improve, sometimes within the same trip.

Is the water warm at Riyue Bay?

Yes — Hainan is a tropical island and this is the world's only wave pool on one. The water is warm year-round, so beginners learn in boardshorts or a swimsuit with a rash guard rather than fighting a thick wetsuit. Wetsuits are available at session pick-up if you want one.
