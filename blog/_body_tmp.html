Surf trip to Hainan from Australia: direct flights, no visa, and a tropical wave pool

One direct flight from Sydney or Melbourne, no visa, a driver waiting at arrivals and the world’s only tropical island wave pool a minute from your room. The Australian surfer’s guide to a Hainan surf and golf trip.

Australian surfers already have the world’s best passport for surf trips: Bali is a short hop, the Mentawais are a boat ride away, and the east coast is on the doorstep. What most of them don’t know is that a tropical Chinese island with a PerfectSwell® wave pool, real ocean surf and the best links course in China is one direct flight from Sydney or Melbourne — and that they can walk in without a visa. This is the surf trip from Australia that nobody else in your crew has done yet.

Fly direct: Sydney and Melbourne to Haikou

Hainan is China’s southernmost province, an island the size of a small country sitting in the South China Sea. It has two international airports, and the one that matters for Australians is Haikou Meilan International (HAK), the main gateway in the north. Sydney and Melbourne both fly direct to Haikou, and so does Auckland for the Kiwis in your group. Leave in the evening, land on a tropical island the next day, and surf that afternoon if you’re keen.

No direct flight on your dates? One stop is all it takes. Hong Kong, Singapore, Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur and Seoul all connect into Haikou or Sanya Phoenix International (SYX), the southern airport in Hainan’s resort zone. Both airports work for Riyue Bay; the right one is whichever has the better flight from your city. Our getting here guide lists every route, and if you send us your departure city we’ll tell you the cleanest option before you buy a ticket.

The time difference is the quiet advantage. Hainan runs on China Standard Time, two hours behind Sydney and Melbourne in the Australian winter and three during daylight saving. Compared with a haul to Europe or the Americas, there is essentially no jet lag — you arrive on roughly the same body clock you left on.

No visa: Australians get 30 days free

This is the part that surprises people. Hainan runs its own visa-free entry policy, separate from the rest of mainland China. Passport holders from Australia and New Zealand — along with the U.S., the UK, Canada and many other countries — enter Hainan visa-free for up to 30 days. No embassy queue in Sydney, no paperwork, no fee. You book the flight, you land, you walk through. We covered the fine print in our Hainan visa-free travel guide, and we confirm every guest’s eligibility at booking.

Thirty days is also far more than a surf trip needs, which means a week at the pool can turn into a fortnight that adds golf, the coast, and a night in Sanya at either end without touching a visa application.

Land, and the trip runs itself

Forward us your e-ticket and the rest is handled. An English-speaking driver waits in the arrivals hall with your name on a sign and a WhatsApp number shared in advance. We track your flight live, so a delay simply moves the pickup. The vehicle is a modern business van — Mercedes V-Class or equivalent — with Wi-Fi, water and room for board bags, seating up to six guests. The transfer is included in every package, and the drive is a highlight in itself: Hainan’s east coast highway runs along the South China Sea, beaches on one side and coconut groves on the other, about two hours from Haikou or one from Sanya.

At the end of the road is Riyue Bay in Wanning, the home of Chinese surfing. Your trip coordinator meets you in the lobby, and the surf hotel sits inside the pool grounds — the room, the breakfast hall, the board room and the lineup are about a minute apart on foot.

One direct flight, no visa, one driver, one coastal drive. That is the entire journey from Australia to a wave that does exactly what you tell it to.

The wave pool no other tropical island has

The PerfectSwell® pool at Riyue Bay is the world’s only wave pool on a tropical island. It runs around twenty wave settings, from mellow beginner rollers to performance walls, barrels and air sections, and it runs on a published schedule year-round. That flips the usual logic of a surf trip: instead of flying to Indonesia and hoping the swell chart holds, you book the session and the wave is there. For a group with mixed ability — a couple of shortboarders, a partner who wants to learn, kids — the pool serves each of them the right wave in the same hour of water. Our PerfectSwell explainer walks through the settings.

The water is tropical and warm, so pack boardshorts, not rubber. Bring your own boards — the van is sized for them — or arrive with hand luggage and pick a performance shortboard, mid-length or soft-top from the rental quiver steps from the pool.

Real ocean surf a short walk down the beach

Unlike almost every other wave pool on earth, this one sits on a real coastline. When the swell is running, Riyue Bay’s ocean break is a short walk from the pool deck: long, organised walls that suit longboards and mid-lengths, on a beach that has hosted WSL competition. Pool laps in the morning, ocean lineup at sunset — the same day, no driving. Our Riyue Bay surf guide covers the ocean side in detail.

Golf that stands up to the waves

Australians who play will recognise the appeal immediately. The Dunes at Shenzhou Peninsula, designed by Tom Weiskopf and Phil Smith, is widely rated the best links course in China — fescue-lined fairways along a South China Sea headland, played in boardshorts weather. Up by Haikou airport, Blackstone at Mission Hills is a former world top-100 layout carved through black lava fields and the stage where Tiger Woods and Rory McIlroy went head to head. Surf the pool in the morning, tee off in the afternoon; our 6-day surf and golf itinerary shows how the week fits together.

When to go from Australia

The pool runs every month, so any window works for sessions. For the full trip — pool plus dependable ocean swell plus comfortable golf weather — the cooler months from roughly October to March are Hainan’s prime season, and they line up neatly with the Australian summer holidays. The one caveat is crowds: Golden Week in early October and Chinese New Year in late January to mid-February are the island’s domestic peaks, so book flights and rooms early if your dates land on them. The Australian winter is Hainan’s hot season, when the pool carries the surfing and dawn and evening sessions are the sweet spot. The rest of the island

Between sessions, Hainan is a tropical China most Australians have never seen: Wanning seafood and Xinglong coffee from plantations a short drive inland, more than two thousand years of island Li culture, empty palm-lined beaches down the coast and, for the rest day, the biggest duty-free mall in the world. It works just as well for a non-surfing partner as it does for the crew chasing clips.

Hainan from Australia — FAQ

Are there direct flights from Australia to Hainan?

Yes. Sydney and Melbourne both have direct flights to Haikou Meilan International (HAK), Hainan's main international gateway, and Auckland flies direct to Haikou as well. From the arrivals hall it is about a two-hour drive down the east coast to Riyue Bay; the private transfer with an English-speaking driver is included in every package. Flying via Hong Kong, Singapore, Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur or Seoul also works, into either Haikou or Sanya (SYX).

Do Australians need a visa for Hainan?

No. Hainan runs its own visa-free entry policy: Australian and New Zealand passport holders — along with travellers from the U.S., the UK, Canada and many other countries — can enter Hainan visa-free for up to 30 days. No embassy visit, no paperwork. We confirm your eligibility when you book.

When is the best time for Australians to surf Hainan?

The PerfectSwell® wave pool runs year-round, so any month works for pool sessions. For the full trip — pool plus dependable ocean swell at Riyue Bay and comfortable golf weather — aim for the cooler months from roughly October to March, which lines up neatly with the Australian summer holidays. Book early if your dates fall on Golden Week in early October or Chinese New Year in late January to mid-February. The Australian winter is Hainan's hot season: the pool carries the surfing, with mornings and evenings the sweet spot.

What is the time difference between Australia and Hainan?

Hainan is on China Standard Time (UTC+8), which is two hours behind Sydney and Melbourne in the Australian winter and three hours behind during daylight saving. That is a small enough shift that most guests surf on their first afternoon without any real jet lag.
